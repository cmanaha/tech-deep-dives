## 2. Entries

**F11-1. The behaviour cloning objective as Ross, Gordon and Bagnell write it (equation 2): the supervised approach finds the policy, among those the learner can represent, with the smallest expected per-state loss under the distribution of states the expert visits.**
- Location: 1011.0686v3 (Ross, Gordon, Bagnell, "A Reduction of Imitation Learning and Structured Prediction to No-Regret Online Learning"), Section 2.1 "Supervised Approach to Imitation", equation (2).
- Verdict: NEW
- Evidence: https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0686v3 | Tier 3, peer-reviewed (AISTATS 2011) | definition | "The traditional approach to imitation learning ignores the change in distribution and simply trains a policy pi that performs well under the distribution of states encountered by the expert d_pi*. This can be achieved using any standard supervised learning algorithm. It finds the policy pi_hat_sup:" then equation (2): pi_hat_sup = arg min over pi in Pi of E_{s ~ d_pi*}[ l(s, pi) ]. (accessed 2026-11-20)
- Use in dive: section 4, the behaviour cloning formula. Symbols: pi a policy, Pi the class of policies the learner can represent, pi* the expert, d_pi* the distribution of states the expert visits, l(s, pi) the loss of pi in state s against the expert's action (the paper's surrogate loss; on the arm, the distance between predicted and recorded target angles), pi_hat_sup the policy training returns. The same expectation, set equal to epsilon, is the premise of theorem 2.1 (F02-3).

**F11-2. ACT's training loss as its Algorithm 1 writes it: L = L_reconst + beta * L_reg, with L_reg the KL divergence from the encoder's distribution over z to a standard Gaussian. The algorithm box labels the reconstruction term MSE; the paper's text in Section IV-C says the reconstruction loss is L1, and LeRobot's code computes L1 (F02-13).**
- Location: 2304.13705v1 (Zhao, Kumar, Levine, Finn, "Learning Fine-Grained Bimanual Manipulation with Low-Cost Hardware"), Algorithm 1 "ACT Training", lines 2 to 11; Section IV-C "Implementing ACT".
- Verdict: NEW (refines F02-8, which attributes the L1 form to Algorithm 1)
- Evidence: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13705v1 | Tier 3, preprint | definition | Algorithm 1: "2: Let a_t, o_t represent action and observation at timestep t, o-bar_t represent o_t without image observations." / "7: Sample z from q_phi(z | a_{t:t+k}, o-bar_t)" / "8: Predict a-hat_{t:t+k} from pi_theta(a-hat_{t:t+k} | o_t, z)" / "9: L_reconst = MSE(a-hat_{t:t+k}, a_{t:t+k})" / "10: L_reg = D_KL(q_phi(z | a_{t:t+k}, o-bar_t) || N(0, I))" / "11: Update theta, phi with ADAM and L = L_reconst + beta L_reg". Section IV-C: "We use L1 loss for reconstruction instead of the more common L2 loss: we noted that L1 loss leads to more precise modeling of the action sequence." Section IV: "Intuitively, higher beta will result in less information transmitted in z". (accessed 2026-11-20)
- Use in dive: section 4, the ACT loss, typeset with the L1 norm for the reconstruction term as the text and the code have it, and one sentence saying the algorithm box prints MSE. Symbols: a_{t:t+k} the demonstrated chunk of k actions from tick t; a-hat_{t:t+k} the predicted chunk; o_t the observation; o-bar_t the observation without images; z the style variable; q_phi the CVAE encoder; N(0, I) the standard Gaussian prior; beta the weight on the second term (LeRobot's `kl_weight = 10.0`, F02-11).

**F11-3. Diffusion Policy's training loss (equation 5): the mean squared error between the noise that was added to a demonstrated action sequence and the noise the network predicts from the observation, the noised sequence and the iteration number.**
- Location: 2303.04137v5 (Chi et al., "Diffusion Policy: Visuomotor Policy Learning via Action Diffusion"), Section 2.2 "DDPM Training" (equation 3) and Section 2.3 "Diffusion for Visuomotor Policy Learning", paragraph "Visual observation conditioning" (equations 4 and 5).
- Verdict: NEW (F02-14 prints the same loss without its equation number; in v5 these equations sit in Section 2, where F02-14 says Sections 3.1 to 3.2)
- Evidence: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04137v5 | Tier 3, preprint | definition | Section 2.2: "The training process starts by randomly drawing unmodified examples, x^0, from the dataset. For each sample, we randomly select a denoising iteration k and then sample a random noise epsilon^k with appropriate variance for iteration k. The noise prediction network is asked to predict the noise from the data sample with noise added." Section 2.3: "The training loss is modified from Eq 3 to:" equation (5): L = MSE(epsilon^k, epsilon_theta(O_t, A_t^0 + epsilon^k, k)). (accessed 2026-11-20)
- Use in dive: section 4, the diffusion policy objective. Symbols: O_t the observation at control step t; A_t^0 the demonstrated (noise-free) action sequence, the superscript counting denoising iterations; k a randomly selected iteration; epsilon^k random noise with the variance for iteration k; epsilon_theta the noise-prediction network.

**F11-4. PPO's clipped surrogate objective (equation 7): L^CLIP(theta) = E_t[ min( r_t(theta) * A_t, clip(r_t(theta), 1 - epsilon, 1 + epsilon) * A_t ) ], where r_t(theta) is the ratio of the new policy's probability of the taken action to the old policy's, A_t is an advantage estimate, and epsilon is a hyperparameter, "say, epsilon = 0.2".**
- Location: 1707.06347v2 (Schulman, Wolski, Dhariwal, Radford, Klimov, "Proximal Policy Optimization Algorithms"), Abstract; Section 2.1 (equation 1 and the definition of the advantage estimator), Section 3 "Clipped Surrogate Objective" (ratio definition, equations 6 and 7), Section 5.
- Verdict: NEW
- Evidence: https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347v2 | Tier 3, preprint | definition | Abstract: "Whereas standard policy gradient methods perform one gradient update per data sample, we propose a novel objective function that enables multiple epochs of minibatch updates." Section 2.1: "where pi_theta is a stochastic policy and A-hat_t is an estimator of the advantage function at timestep t. Here, the expectation E-hat_t[...] indicates the empirical average over a finite batch of samples, in an algorithm that alternates between sampling and optimization." Section 3: "Let r_t(theta) denote the probability ratio r_t(theta) = pi_theta(a_t | s_t) / pi_theta_old(a_t | s_t), so r(theta_old) = 1." and "The main objective we propose is the following:" equation (7), "where epsilon is a hyperparameter, say, epsilon = 0.2." and "The second term, clip(r_t(theta), 1 - epsilon, 1 + epsilon) A-hat_t, modifies the surrogate objective by clipping the probability ratio, which removes the incentive for moving r_t outside of the interval [1 - epsilon, 1 + epsilon]. Finally, we take the minimum of the clipped and unclipped objective, so the final objective is a lower bound (i.e., a pessimistic bound) on the unclipped objective. With this scheme, we only ignore the change in probability ratio when it would make the objective improve, and we include it when it makes the objective worse." Section 5: "Most techniques for computing variance-reduced advantage-function estimators make use a learned state-value function V(s)". (accessed 2026-11-20)
- Use in dive: section 8, the PPO update in the simulation lane. Symbols: s_t the state at step t, a_t the action taken, pi_theta the policy with the current weights, theta_old the weights before the update (the ones that collected the batch), r_t the probability ratio, A-hat_t the advantage estimate, epsilon the clip range, E-hat_t the average over the batch. The SO-101 recipe sets `clip_param = 0.2` (F04-58) and piRL lists "Clip ratio epsilon 0.2" (F04-69).
