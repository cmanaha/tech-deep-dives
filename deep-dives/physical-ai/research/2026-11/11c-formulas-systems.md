**F11-200. MuJoCo's general equations of motion in continuous time are M v-dot + c = tau + J^T f, where v is the joint velocity, tau the applied force (passive, actuation, external), c the bias force (Coriolis, centrifugal, gravitational), M the inertia in joint space, J the constraint Jacobian and f the constraint force. The transpose of the Jacobian maps a constraint force to a force in joint coordinates.**

**F11-203. **F11-210. MuJoCo's implicit-in-velocity update is v_{t+h} = v_t + h M-hat^{-1} M a(v_t), with M-hat = M + h D and D the negated derivative of the velocity-dependent forces with respect to velocity. The first three single-step integrators (`Euler`, `implicit`, `implicitfast`) use this update with different definitions of D. For `implicitfast`, D holds the derivatives of all forces the implicit method uses except the centripetal and Coriolis forces, and is symmetrised. The position line q_{t+h} = q_t + h v_{t+h} is unchanged. The plain semi-implicit update of F11-201 is used by `Euler` only when the model has no joint damping or the `eulerdamp` flag is disabled.**
- Location: MuJoCo docs, Computation chapter, "Numerical integration", equation labelled `eq_implicit_update`, and "Integrators" (`doc/computation/index.rst` at tag 4.0.1)

**F11-211. In MuJoCo the constraint force f covers four constraint types: equality, friction loss, limit and contact. Friction loss (dry friction in a joint) is modelled as a constraint, an upper limit on the absolute value of the friction force, set by the `frictionloss` attribute of a joint or tendon. Limits are handled by the solver as frictionless contacts.**
- Location: MuJoCo docs, Computation chapter, "Constraint model", opening paragraph and "Friction loss" (`doc/computation/index.rst` at tag 4.0.1)
- Verdict: NEW
