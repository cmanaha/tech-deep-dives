### G2. Does RL-trained self-correction (SCoRe) add an ability prompting lacks, by how much, on which model? (wave 2 question 2 / UNKNOWN U8, register 03 U4)
- Finding: yes, on two Gemini models, two benchmarks, and one round of correction. On MATH with Gemini 1.5 Flash, prompting-based self-correction (Self-Refine) loses 1.0 point between first and second attempt and the base model with the correction instruction loses 11.2; SCoRe gains 4.4. On HumanEval with Gemini 1.0 Pro, Self-Refine loses 1.2, the base gains 3.0, SCoRe gains 12.2. Fine-tuning on offline correction traces (STaR, Pair-SFT) gives +0.4 and +1.8 on MATH. SCoRe also raises the first attempt (52.6% to 60.0% on MATH), so its higher second-attempt accuracy (64.4% against Self-Refine's 51.8%) mixes better first answers with better corrections.
  - https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12917v2 | Tier 3, preprint (no venue in the arXiv comments field; latest version v2) | benchmark claim | Table 2 (MATH): "Base model 52.6% 41.4% -11.2%"; "Self-Refine (Madaan et al., 2023) 52.8% 51.8% -1.0%"; "STaR w/ D_StaR+ 53.6% 54.0% 0.4%"; "Pair-SFT w/ D_SFT 52.4% 54.2% 1.8%"; "SCoRe (Ours) 60.0% 64.4% 4.4%" (columns Acc.@t1, Acc.@t2, Δ(t1, t2)). Table 3 (HumanEval): "Base model 47.3% 53.7% 56.7% 3.0%"; "Self-Refine 30.7% 53.7% 52.5% -1.2%"; "SCoRe (Ours) 60.6% 52.4% 64.6% 12.2%".
