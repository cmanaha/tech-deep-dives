### A2. Pipeline 1a: DeepSeek-R1 (arXiv 2501.12948v2, v2 dated 4 Jan 2026; published in Nature)

Choice of the four pipelines (item A2): (1) DeepSeek, R1 plus the newer V4 (1a, 1b), (2) Moonshot Kimi K3, (3) Mistral Ministral 3, (4) AI2 Olmo 3. Between them they show human-written and teacher-generated SFT, logit distillation, preference optimisation (DPO, online DPO), RL with verifiable rewards, RL with reward models and rubric judges, and on-policy distillation. Qwen3 (item A3) is recorded separately below and shows strong-to-weak distillation.

Version check: the arXiv abstract page lists two versions, "[v1] Wed, 22 Jan 2025" and "[v2] Sun, 4 Jan 2026"; v2 is the latest and was read. Journal reference on the abstract page: "Nature volume 645, pages 633-638 (2025)".

**F07-1. DeepSeek-R1's pipeline runs four stages on top of DeepSeek-V3-Base: a small cold-start SFT on human-styled long chain-of-thought, a first RL stage, rejection sampling plus SFT on reasoning and non-reasoning data, and a second RL stage for helpfulness and harmlessness.**

- Verdict: HOLDS
- Evidence: https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948v2 (accessed 2026-11-07) | Tier 3 (Nature 2025, per arXiv journal-ref) | pipeline description | "In the initial stage, we collect thousands of cold-start data that exhibits a conversational, human-aligned thinking process. This stage incorporates both reasoning and non-reasoning datasets into the SFT process, enabling the model to not only excel in reasoning tasks but also demonstrate advanced writing capabilities. To further align the model with human preferences, we implement a secondary RL stage designed to enhance the model's helpfulness and harmlessness while simultaneously refining its reasoning capabilities." Base: "DeepSeek-R1 and DeepSeek-R1-Zero are trained on top of DeepSeek-V3-Base and DeepSeek-R1 leverages non-reasoning data from DeepSeek-V3 SFT data. - Location: Section 1 (introduction summary); Section 5.1 "Post-Training Pipeline"; 5.1.1 "Specialist Training".
- Verdict: NEW (a newer DeepSeek pipeline than R1, recorded alongside it as the brief asks)

The base model first undergoes Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on high-quality, domain-specific data to establish foundational capabilities. Subsequently, Reinforcement Learning (RL) is applied using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (DeepSeek-AI, 2025a), which further optimizes the model for domain-aligned behaviors guided by reward models tailored to specific success criteria. **F07-11. DeepSeek-V4 drops scalar reward models: hard-to-verify tasks use rubric-guided data scored by a generative reward model, and the actor itself is trained as that judge.**
- Location: Section 5.1.1, "Generative Reward Model" paragraph.
- Verdict: NEW
- Evidence: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19348v1 (accessed 2026-11-07) | Tier 3 (preprint) | method description and author claim | "Typically, easy-to-verify tasks can be effectively optimized using simple rule-based verifiers or test cases. In contrast, hard-to-verify tasks traditionally rely on Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), which necessitates extensive human annotation to train a scalar reward model. In the post-training phase of DeepSeek-V4 series, however, we dispense with these conventional scalar-based reward models. Instead, to address hard-to-verify tasks, we curate rubric-guided RL data and employ a Generative Reward Model (GRM) to evaluate policy trajectories. Crucially, we apply RL optimization directly to the GRM itself. In this paradigm, the actor network natively functions as the GRM" ... - Use in dive: section 3 signal map (verifier, rubric plus LLM judge, scalar RM: three reward sources). [MM]

**F07-12. DeepSeek-V4's OPD objective: a weighted sum of reverse KL divergences from the student to each of more than ten expert teachers, computed on the student's own samples, using full-vocabulary logits rather than the per-token sampled estimate used as an RL advantage.**
- Location: Section 5.1.2 "On-Policy Distillation", Eq. 29.
- Verdict: NEW
- Evidence: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19348v1 (accessed 2026-11-07) | Tier 3 (preprint) | definition and method | "This is achieved by having the student learn from the output distributions of teacher models on its own generated trajectories." Eq. 29 in plain text: "L_OPD(θ) = sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i · D_KL(π_θ || π_Ei)". "In this formulation, w_i represents the assigned weight for each expert, typically determined by the relative importance of the expert. Computing the reverse KL loss D_KL(π_θ || π_Ei) requires sampling training trajectories from the student π_θ to maintain on-policy learning." "In this stage, more than ten teacher models covering various domains are employed to distill a single student model." "prior works usually simplify the full-vocabulary KL loss into a token-level KL estimate at each token position, and reuse RL framework by replacing sg[log π_Ei(y_t|x,y<t) / π_θ(y_t|x,y<t)] (sg represents the stop gradient operation) as the per-token advantage estimate in the policy loss calculation. Although this approach is resource-efficient, it leads to high variance in gradient estimation and often causes training instability. Therefore, we adopt full-vocabulary logit distillation in our OPD."
- Use in dive: section 11 (OPD formula: student samples, teacher grades every token; the per-token log-ratio as an "advantage" ties OPD to the policy-gradient section). [MM] [PR]

### B4. Hinton, Vinyals and Dean 2015, "Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network" (arXiv 1503.02531v1)

**F07-30. Distillation trains a small model on the large model's class probabilities ("soft targets"); with high entropy, soft targets carry much more information per example than a one-hot label, and give lower-variance gradients.**
- Location: Section 1 "Introduction".
- Verdict: HOLDS
- Evidence: https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02531v1 (accessed 2026-11-07) | Tier 3 (NIPS 2014 Deep Learning Workshop, per arXiv comment) | definition and argument | "An obvious way to transfer the generalization ability of the cumbersome model to a small model is to use the class probabilities produced by the cumbersome model as "soft targets" for training the small model." "When the soft targets have high entropy, they provide much more information per training case than hard targets and much less variance in the gradient between training cases, so the small model can often be trained on much less data than the original cumbersome model and using a much higher learning rate." Worked example: "one version of a 2 may be given a probability of 10^-6 of being a 3 and 10^-9 of being a 7 whereas for another version it may be the other way around. - Use in dive: section 4 (why a teacher's full distribution beats a label; the "dark knowledge" intuition before the formula). [MM]

**F07-31. The softmax with temperature T: q_i = exp(z_i/T) / sum_j exp(z_j/T); higher T gives a softer distribution. Teacher and student use the same high T during distillation; the student uses T = 1 afterwards.**
- Location: Section 2 "Distillation", Eq. 1.
- Verdict: HOLDS
- Evidence: https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02531v1 (accessed 2026-11-07) | Tier 3 (NIPS 2014 Deep Learning Workshop) | definition | "Neural networks typically produce class probabilities by using a "softmax" output layer that converts the logit, z_i, computed for each class into a probability, q_i, by comparing z_i with the other logits." Eq. 1 in plain text: "q_i = exp(z_i/T) / sum_j exp(z_j/T)" "where T is a temperature that is normally set to 1. Using a higher value for T produces a softer probability distribution over classes. In the simplest form of distillation, knowledge is transferred to the distilled model by training it on a transfer set and using a soft target distribution for each case in the transfer set that is produced by using the cumbersome model with a high temperature in its softmax. The same high temperature is used when training the distilled model, but after it has been trained it uses a temperature of 1."
- Use in dive: section 4 (formula, then decomposition: T flattens the teacher so small probabilities matter in the loss). [MM]

**F07-32. Combining soft and hard targets: a weighted average of cross-entropy with the soft targets (at temperature T) and with the true labels (at T = 1), with a much lower weight on the labels, and the soft-target gradients multiplied by T^2.**
- Location: Section 2 "Distillation".
- Verdict: NEW
- Evidence: https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02531v1 (accessed 2026-11-07) | Tier 3 (NIPS 2014 Deep Learning Workshop) | method | "we found that a better way is to simply use a weighted average of two different objective functions. The first objective function is the cross entropy with the soft targets and this cross entropy is computed using the same high temperature in the softmax of the distilled model as was used for generating the soft targets from the cumbersome model. The second objective function is the cross entropy with the correct labels. This is computed using exactly the same logits in softmax of the distilled model but at a temperature of 1. We found that the best results were generally obtained by using a condiderably lower weight on the second objective function. Since the magnitudes of the gradients produced by the soft targets scale as 1/T^2 it is important to multiply them by T^2 when using both hard and soft targets." (sic "condiderably")
- Use in dive: section 4 [PR] (the T^2 factor is the detail people miss); ties to Meta's "dynamically weights the soft and hard targets" (F07-21). [PR]
