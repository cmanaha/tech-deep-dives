### 3.3 Does the model state fit on one node? [derived]

Inputs: Llama 3.1 70B has 70,553,706,496 parameters and 8B has 8,030,261,248 (T01-3); mixed-precision Adam holds 16 bytes per parameter (T01-9); HBM per node from section 3.1 (GiB x 1.0737 = GB).

### 3.5 Storage throughput for one 70B checkpoint [derived]

Inputs: 1,128.9 GB of model state (section 3.3); FSx PERSISTENT-1000 at 1,000 MBps per TiB (T05-35); per-client ceiling 1,200 Gbps with EFA and GDS (T05-36).

- 100 TiB of PERSISTENT-1000 gives about 100 GB/s aggregate: a full 70B state save takes about 11.3 s if the file system is the bottleneck.
- Spread over 32 p5.48xlarge clients, each writes 35.3 GB; at the 150 GB/s per-client EFA-with-GDS ceiling that is 0.24 s, so the file system's aggregate throughput, not the client link, sets the save time. Area 04 owns checkpoint frequency and asynchronous saving.

## 4. Mental models worth teaching

1. **The instance is the scale-up domain, and the domain sets where tensor parallelism can live.** Eight GPUs on NVSwitch or NVLink for P4 to P6-B300, 36 or 72 GPUs on P6e-GB200, 16 chips on a 4x4 torus for trn2 and 64 on its UltraServer, 64 or 144 on a NeuronSwitch for Trn3 (T05-2 to T05-12). Everything outside the domain crosses EFA at 400 Gbps per accelerator on P5 and P6-B200, 800 Gbps on P6-B300 and P6e, 200 Gbps per Trainium2 or Trainium3 chip [derived, section 3.1].
2. **New silicon goes to low precision and memory, not BF16.** B300 has B200's BF16 and FP8 peaks with 50% more HBM (T05-15); Trainium3 has Trainium2's BF16 peak and doubles only the MXFP8 rate (T05-18); H200 is H100 compute with 76% more HBM (T05-14). A BF16 run on the newer part gains memory headroom, not peak FLOPS.
3. **For P and Trn, future capacity is a Capacity Block or a training plan.** Quotas start at 0 vCPUs (T05-31); future-dated ODCRs exclude P and Trn (T05-28); Capacity Blocks and training plans reserve up to 8 weeks ahead, for up to 182 days, ending at 11:30 UTC (T05-26, T05-29).
4. **A Capacity Block is a schedule, not a premium.** On 2026-10-30 every us-east-1 block rate is at or below On-Demand, from 0.998 (P5en, P6-B200) down to 0.443 (Trn1) [derived, T05-22]; you pay up front for certainty of the window and lose cancellation and placement groups (T05-27).
5. **The managed premium is a flat multiplier.** SageMaker training jobs cost 1.15 times EC2 On-Demand, HyperPod nodes 1.20 times on P5 and P6, training plans 1.15 times the Capacity Block rate (T05-23, T05-24).
6. **Storage throughput is bought with capacity.** FSx for Lustre aggregate throughput is TiB x tier (T05-35), while each client is capped by its interface: 100 Gbps without EFA, 700 with EFA, 1,200 with GDS (T05-36); trn2 is excluded from the EFA path (T05-37).
7. **Images split by layer.** The AMI owns kernel driver, NVIDIA driver and EFA kernel module; the container owns NCCL, aws-ofi-nccl and libfabric; on EKS the device plugins are a third, separate install (T05-43 to T05-45).

## 5. Contradictions

**C05-1. Trainium2 accelerator memory.** The EC2 specification page lists trn2.48xlarge accelerator memory as "8192 GiB (16 x 512 GiB)" and trn2.3xlarge as "512 GiB (1 x 512 GiB)" (https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ec2/latest/instancetypes/ac.html). The Neuron documentation gives "96 GiB of device memory" per chip and 1,536 GiB per trn2.48xlarge (https://awsdocs-neuron.readthedocs-hosted.com/en/latest/about-neuron/arch/neuron-hardware/trainium2.html, trn2-arch.html), and the Trn2 product page gives "96 GB" and "1.5 TB" (https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/instance-types/trn2/). Two first-party sources agree on 96 GiB per chip; the specification page cell is wrong. The EFA dive's August 2026 research recorded the same cell, so it has persisted at least three months. All accessed 2026-10-30.

**C05-2. Blackwell HBM per GPU depends on the page and the unit.** AWS specification page: p6-b200 "1432 GiB (8 x 179 GiB)", p6e-gb200 "740 GiB (4 x 185 GiB)", p6-b300 "2148 GiB (8 x 268 GiB)". P6 product page: "1440 GB" in prose and "1,432 HBM3e" in the GB column for p6-b200; "2,144" for p6-b300 against "2148 GiB" on the specification page. NVIDIA: HGX B200 "Total Memory 1.4 TB", GB200 superchip "372 GB HBM3E" (186 GB per GPU). Calculators should take the AWS specification page's GiB per GPU, which is what the instance exposes [our reading]; the product page mixes GB and GiB.

**C05-3. Trainium3 NeuronLink bandwidth per chip has three first-party values.** Neuron "Trainium3 Architecture": "NeuronLink-v4 ... provides 2.56 TB/sec bandwidth per device". Neuron "Amazon EC2 Trn3 Architecture" table: "NeuronLink-v4 bandwidth (GiB/sec/device) | 2,048 | 2,048", with a link breakdown of 256 GB/s intra-server, 320 GB/s inter-server and 128 GB/s inter-rack per chip (bidirectional). Trn3 product page: "NeuronLink-v4 with 2TB/s of bandwidth per chip". No code settles this; the dive should quote the per-level breakdown, which is what a collective actually traverses, and state the disagreement.

**C05-4. Trn2 UltraServer status.** The UltraServers page says Trn2 instances "in a Trn2 UltraServer configuration (available in preview)" (https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/ultraservers/); the Trn2 product page says "Trn2 UltraServers are available now" (https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/instance-types/trn2/), and the Capacity Blocks UltraServer table lists Trn2 in us-east-2 (T05-46). The UltraServers page is stale. The UltraClusters page likewise lists instances only up to P6-B200 (T05-33).

**C05-5. PCS support for P6e UltraServers.** P6 product page: "(P6e-GB200 and P6e-GB300 support coming soon)". AWS What's New, 18 June 2026: "AWS Parallel Computing Service (PCS) now supports Amazon EC2 P6e-GB200 and P6e-GB300 UltraServer instances" (T05-8). The product page is STALE at the 2026-06-18 boundary.

**C05-6. p5e.48xlarge Capacity Blocks in us-east-1.** The Capacity Blocks region table marks p5e.48xlarge as supported in us-east-1 (T05-46); the Capacity Blocks pricing page lists p5e rates in Ohio, N. California, Oregon, the Phoenix Local Zone and seven non-US Regions but not N. Virginia (T05-22); the us-east-1 EC2 price file has no p5e rows at all (T05-21). Two sources against one; treat p5e in us-east-1 as unconfirmed (U05-7).

**C05-7. How a Capacity Block is priced.** The billing page says "The price of a Capacity Block depends on available supply and demand for Capacity Blocks at the time of purchase" and that a date-range search returns "the lowest-priced Capacity Block offering available"; the pricing page publishes one rate per type and Region, "updated regularly" with the next update "in January, 2027" (T05-22, T05-27). Our reading: the published rate is a schedule updated in steps, and the offering's quoted price at purchase is the binding number; quote the offering, cite the page for orientation.

## 7. Reading path for a newcomer

1. EC2 accelerated instance specification page (https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ec2/latest/instancetypes/ac.html): the one table of record for accelerator count, HBM, network cards and NVMe; read it first and treat its Trainium2 memory cell with care (C05-1).
2. The P5 and P6 product pages: the intra-node interconnect the specification page omits, and the UltraServer shapes (T05-2 to T05-7).
3. NVIDIA HGX and GB200 NVL72 specification pages, then the Neuron Trainium2 and Trainium3 pages: the peak FLOPS that every MFU figure divides by, with the sparse-versus-dense footnotes (T05-13 to T05-19).
4. EC2 Capacity Blocks for ML (overview, purchase, billing), then On-Demand Capacity Reservations and the On-Demand quota table: how capacity is actually obtained (T05-26 to T05-31).
5. SageMaker training plans page, then the Price List files for EC2 and SageMaker: the managed equivalents and their premiums (T05-23, T05-24, T05-29).
6. FSx for Lustre performance, SSD storage classes and EFA-enabled file systems; then S3 performance and S3 Express One Zone; then Mountpoint's SEMANTICS.md: the storage path and its ceilings (T05-35 to T05-42). The EFA dive's StorageDataPaths section next for the mechanism.
7. DLAMI base release notes, DLC image configs and the EKS accelerated AMI page: what software each layer brings (T05-43 to T05-45).
