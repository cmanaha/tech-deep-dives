# SGLang Scope Review: Newcomer Lens

Reviewer stance: a technical lead (ex-EC2/compute, solutions architect) who has read the sibling vLLM dive, has never run SGLang, and reads on a phone and a laptop. Independent of the scope's author.

Scope reviewed: `deep-dives/sglang/SCOPE.md` (SGLang v0.5.22, 29 planned sections).

Standards applied: root `CLAUDE.md` (Section Shape, Content Philosophy, Acronym Standard), `deep-dives/efa/revamp/section-shape-standard.md`, and the Getting Started rule (audience, 2 to 4 prerequisite concepts with where each is defined, order of mental models and why, how sections feed each other).

## Verdict

For a vLLM-literate technical lead, the scope is sound in substance. Each section has a stated mental model or a practical action, the evidence is pinned, and the hedges keep their scope. No section fails the governing test outright, though section 13 is thin and section 27 is mostly recap. The reader's problems are about route, not content. Getting Started meets requirement 1 (who it is for). It partly meets requirement 2: it lists four prerequisites but leaves out vLLM's "6. Automatic Prefix Caching", which the central model is contrasted against. It partly meets requirement 3: it orders section numbers rather than mental models. It does not meet requirement 4 in prose. All three reading routes skip prerequisites of sections they include. The AWS route is the worst: it reaches 24 (PD and wide EP over EFA) without PD, wide EP or the radix cache. Six concepts arrive one or more sections before they are defined: SGLang's pools, lpm, tree speculation, bootstrap rooms, PD roles in the wide-EP recipe, and resolution hooks. The vLLM-to-SGLang term mapping (retraction for preemption, DeepEP normal for high-throughput, TBO for DBO, `--mem-fraction-static` against `--gpu-memory-utilization`) is never stated where the reader needs it. Sections 22 and 27 give contradictory API-key advice for SageMaker. Apply the swaps below, the Getting Started rewrite (#36 to #42) and the term bridges (#5 to #8) before building. The rest are sentence-level fixes that authors can apply during drafting.
