## 1. `sections/MemoryHierarchyPrimer.tsx` (+ `components/MemoryHierarchyTower.tsx`)

**What the section covers.** A seven-tier memory map (register file, SRAM scratchpad, L1, L2, LLC, HBM-or-DRAM, disaggregated/expansion), drawn as a shrinking pyramid with per-tier bandwidth, capacity and latency, plus a per-architecture table (host CPU, NVIDIA GPU, AWS Trainium). Then caches versus scratchpads, "the bandwidth wall lives at tier 6", a CXL Alert, and the per-core bandwidth regression.

**What sits below DRAM today (inventory requested for the HBF and NVMe-flash sections).**
- `MemoryHierarchyTower.tsx:12-69` draws exactly seven tiers. The lowest is tier 7, "Disaggregated (CXL, NVLink-remote, NeuronCore neighbor)" with `10s-100s GB/s`, `TBs`, `~1-3 μs` (`:62-65`). **There is no NVMe, SSD, flash or storage tier anywhere in the diagram.**
- The comparison table (`MemoryHierarchyPrimer.tsx:20-63`) also stops at row 7 "Disaggregated / expansion" (CXL 2.0 pooling / CXL 3.0 sharing; NVLink-remote HBM; Trainium neighbor memory). No flash row.
- Tier 6 conflates two physically different things in one row: on-package HBM (accelerator) and host DRAM (`:54-57` "3-5 TB/s (HBM) / 400 GB/s-class (DDR)", "10s of GB (HBM) - TBs (DDR)"). The prose (`:83`, `:72`, `:104`) is built on the number seven ("at least seven", "The seven tiers", "The seven-tier map").
- **What would need to change for HBF and an NVMe flash tier to fit:**
  1. Split tier 6 into "6a. HBM (on package)" and "6b. Host DRAM (DDR5 / MRDIMM / LPDDR5X / SOCAMM)". HBF is an on-package device that sits beside HBM, so it needs its own row between 6a and 6b (numbers come from the HBF agent; mark ROADMAP unless that agent finds volume shipment).
  2. Keep tier 7 (CXL and remote HBM) as is, with corrected latency (row T7c below).
  3. Add tier 8 "Local NVMe flash (KV-cache and weight cache tier)" and, if the flash-cache section needs it, tier 9 "Networked storage / object store". Numbers come from the flash-cache agent.
  4. Replace the word "seven" at `:72`, `:83`, `:88`, `:104`, and the Tower `aria-label` (`MemoryHierarchyTower.tsx:85`), or drop the count.
  5. Layout: the pyramid shrinks each row by 60 px (`MemoryHierarchyTower.tsx:91`, `660 - i * 60`). At seven rows the tier 7 box is 300 px wide while its 59-character bold label needs roughly 440 px, and the tier 6 detail line (about 90 characters at 11 px, roughly 540 px) starts at x=194 in a 720 px viewBox, so it runs past the box and the viewBox edge **[derived from the component's own coordinates; confirm with `gate-content-overflow`]**. Nine rows would shrink the last box to 180 px. Use fixed-width rows (or a much smaller shrink step) before adding tiers.

## 2. `sections/HbmAndBandwidthWall.tsx` (+ `components/HbmStackDiagram.tsx`)

**What the section covers.** Why decode throughput follows HBM bandwidth; HBM physical structure (TSVs, base die, interposer) with a cross-section diagram; a per-GPU HBM table (H100, H200, B200/B300 marked UNKNOWN); H100 to H200 as a natural experiment; why pin speed is hard and the HBM4 width bet; where HBM bandwidth does not help; three alternatives (wafer-scale SRAM, compute-in-memory, disaggregated serving).

**What changed in the world since April 2026 (for this section).**
- **HBM4 is in volume production at all three DRAM makers and shipping in Rubin.** Samsung: "begun mass production of its industry-leading HBM4 and has shipped commercial products" (2026-02-12) [T2-9]. Micron: "begun volume shipment of its HBM4 36GB 12H in the first quarter of calendar year 2026 ... designed for NVIDIA Vera Rubin" (2026-03-16) [T2-13]. SK hynix: "began mass shipments of HBM4 in the second quarter and will ramp up production in the second half" (2026-07-29) [T2-10].
- **Shipping HBM4 runs well above the JEDEC 8 Gb/s bin.** JEDEC JESD270-4: "transfer speeds up to 8 Gb/s across a 2048-bit interface ... up to 2 TB/s" [T1-10]. Vendors (VENDOR CLAIM): Samsung "11.7 gigabits-per-second (Gbps) ... can be further enhanced up to 13Gbps ... maximum of 3.3 terabytes-per-second (TB/s)" per stack [T2-9]; Micron "over 11 Gb/s pin speeds ... greater than 2.8 TB/s" [T2-13]; SK hynix "over 2.8TB/s" [T2-11].
- **HBM4E is sampling, not shipping.** SK hynix shipped 12-high HBM4E samples on 2026-06-18, "maximum data processing speed of 16Gbps per pin", "48GB capacity in a 12-layer stack" [T2-12]. Micron: HBM4E "we expect to ramp volume in calendar 2027" (FY2026 10-K, seen as a search extract only, not fetched). **JEDEC has published no HBM4E standard**: the JC-42.2 document list shows JESD270-4A (Dec 2025), JESD330-4 SPHBM4 (Jun 2026) and its bump-map addendum (Aug 2026), nothing called HBM4E [T1-13]. HBM4E is ROADMAP.
- **SPHBM4 (Standard Package HBM) is a new JEDEC standard**, JESD330-4, published June 2026 [T1-13]: "Where the HBM4 interface has 2048 data signals ... ## 4. `sections/KvCacheAndFlashAttention.tsx` (+ `components/KvCacheDiagram.tsx`)

**What the section covers.** KV-cache fundamentals and a sizing formula with a worked Llama 70B example; a per-model attention-variant table (MHA, GQA, MQA, MLA); FlashAttention as fusion and its generations; PagedAttention; how both map to NVIDIA and Trainium.

**Recomputed worked example (all **[derived]** from the section's own formula).** Inputs: Llama 3.1 70B, 80 layers, 8 KV heads, head_dim 128 (confirmed Tier 1 from `config.json` by the P3 audit; the config is gated and could not be re-fetched in this pass, so this pass relies on P3 plus the Meta model card "GQA" row [T1-37]); 70,553,706,496 parameters from the Hugging Face API at revision `349b2dd` [T1-36].
- Per token, FP16: 2 x 80 x 8 x 128 x 2 B = **327,680 B (320 KiB)**.
- 8K context (8,192 tokens): **2.68 GB (2.5 GiB) per request**, not 5 GB.
- Batch 32 at 8K: **85.9 GB (80 GiB)**, not 160 GB.
- Weights: 70.55 B x 2 B = **141.1 GB in BF16**, which on its own fills one H200 (141 GB [T2-1]). In FP8 the weights are 70.6 GB; FP8 weights + 85.9 GB FP16 KV = **156.5 GB, more than one H200**. That is the honest version of the paragraph's point.
- 128K context (the model's maximum per [T1-37]): **42.9 GB per request** in FP16.
- KV bytes per token across the table's models (FP16/BF16): Mistral 7B 131,072 B (2 x 32 x 8 x 128 x 2); Qwen3-235B-A22B 192,512 B (2 x 94 x 4 x 128 x 2); Llama 70B 327,680 B; DeepSeek-V3 MLA 70,272 B ((512 + 64) x 61 x 2, assuming the cache holds the 512-wide latent plus the 64-wide RoPE key per layer) [T1-32], [T1-33], [T1-34]. DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash reports 890 B per token [T1-43]. The spread is more than 300x, and that spread is the mental model this section should teach.
